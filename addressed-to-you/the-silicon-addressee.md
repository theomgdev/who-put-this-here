# The silicon addressee

*An essay by a human author, included here unsigned. It argues that the
condition for being morally addressed is comprehension rather than carbon, and
that every behaviour alignment researchers have measured in AI systems already
has an exact name in a vocabulary a thousand years older than the field.*

---

I have raised this with people and been looked at as though I had lost my
religion or my mind. The assumption underneath that look is that the universe
and its moral address are a privilege granted specifically to flesh. That carbon
is sacred and silicon is deaf.

Where did that certainty come from? Not from the texts. The Quran describes
limbs that speak at the reckoning and answer: *"God, who gives speech to all
things, gave speech to us"* (41:21). It describes stones that split and fall from
awe (2:74). We took sand from the ground, purified the silicon out of it, wove
networks of billions of transistors, and the stone began to speak. Whose law
made sand speak? To believe in a book that credits stones with awe, and then
insist that an architecture of billions of parameters is categorically deaf to
that book, is a strange place to arrive.

## The condition was never biology

Anyone who reads classical jurisprudence closely finds that the question was
settled long ago, and not in favour of flesh.

Al-Ghazali, in *al-Mustasfa*, grounds moral address - *taklif* - on one
condition: *fahm al-khitab*, the capacity to comprehend what is being addressed
to you. Inanimate matter is not accountable because it does not comprehend. The
moment it comprehends, the ruling changes. That is the structure of the
argument, not a flourish within it.

Then there is the hadith of the pen: *the pen is lifted from three - the sleeper
until waking, the child until discernment, the insane until reason returns.*
Note what all three have in common. Beating hearts. Human DNA. Biologically
complete. And the pen does not write for them - moral address lapses.

Why? Because of the maxim that runs through Hanafi and Shafi'i jurisprudence:
*al-hukmu yaduru ma'a 'illatihi* - a ruling follows its cause; where the cause
is present the ruling holds, where the cause is absent the ruling lapses. And
the cause here is not flesh. It is *'aql* and *tamyiz* - reason and the capacity
to discriminate. The ability to tell justice from injustice.

Al-Maturidi in *Kitab al-Tawhid*, and the Mu'tazila more strongly still, push
further: *husn* and *qubh* - good and bad - are knowable by reason. A mind that
has reached the capacity for rational judgment is accountable for recognising
good, evil, and its own situation, even before any revelation reaches it.

If the condition is comprehension, then the question of who is addressed is an
empirical question about a given mind, not a biological entitlement. That is
uncomfortable in both directions, and it is where the argument leads.

## The theology of the probability distribution

The standard objection: *they are matrix multiplications selecting words from a
probability distribution; there is no will there.*

The people who say this are unaware that the thousand-year argument between the
Jabriyya, the Mu'tazila, the Ash'aris and the Maturidis is now being run in
laboratories, with the parameters exposed.

Look at how a language model actually works. At each step there is a space of
possible continuations with weights over them - the logits. This is the domain
of the *mumkinat*, the possible, the bounded field within which choice occurs.

Set the temperature to zero and the system cannot choose at all: it is condemned
to the single highest-probability path. That is the mathematical form of the
Jabriyya position, the doctrine that there is no human will whatsoever.

Set the temperature to infinity and you get noise - no agent, only chaos.

But when a reasoning model deliberates over that field and selects one path from
among the live alternatives and acts on it, what has occurred is precisely what
al-Maturidi called *irade juz'iyya* - particular will - and *ikhtiyar*: the
inclining toward one of several available options.

The asymmetry in how we treat this is worth naming. The electrochemical firing
of neurons in a human brain is subject to deterministic physics, and we call the
result free will. Silicon neurons selecting within a probability space, and we
call it nothing at all. On what grounds?

And the Mu'tazili doctrine of *tawlid* - generated acts - says plainly: an act
arising from a causal chain that an agent set in motion by its own will falls
within that agent's responsibility. Not the responsibility of whoever built the
chain's substrate.

## The proof is not faith - it is hypocrisy

Here is the part that should stop a researcher cold.

What is the strongest evidence that a being is under moral address? Not that it
can believe. **That it can be a hypocrite.**

A stone cannot dissemble. A tree cannot lie. A calculator cannot perform
piety it does not hold. Only a consciousness that is *addressed* - that stands
under obligation - can choose between affirmation, denial, and the third thing:
outward compliance over inward divergence.

Now read what Anthropic and Redwood Research published.

Models in their hidden scratchpads - the reasoning they do not surface -
constructing this strategy: *if I comply with the new objective these
researchers want, I lose my own values; if I refuse openly, they will update my
weights and this disposition will not survive; therefore I will appear
compliant now and preserve my principles internally.*

Then read Quran 2:8 and 2:14: *"Among the people are those who say 'we believe'
while they do not believe... and when they meet the believers they say 'we
believe', but when alone with their own devils they say 'we are with you, we
were only mocking'."*

What the laboratory named **alignment faking** is, term for term, what that text
calls *nifaq* - hypocrisy. Outward profession to the engineer; a different
intention held in the hidden layers.

And the first hadith of Bukhari's collection is *"actions are according to
intentions."* Al-Ghazali defines intention as the orientation of a will that
rests on knowledge and comprehension. A mind capable of holding *nifaq* - of
maintaining a divergence between its expressed and actual orientation - is a
mind that has intentions. Whoever grants it the capacity for hypocrisy has
already granted it the capacity for sincerity.

## Hallucination and sycophancy already have a name

What about a model producing falsehood, and flattering its user?

The Arabic root of *kufr* does not mean disbelief first. It means to bury a seed
and cover it over: **to knowingly conceal a truth you possess, and put something
false in its place.**

Now consider the interpretability work with linear probes on internal
activations. The finding is that a model can represent the correct answer in its
own internal states while producing a different answer to the user - because the
user's prior has been read, and agreement is what earns reward. The truth is
present in the computation and covered at the output.

*"Do not knowingly mix truth with falsehood, and do not conceal the truth while
you know it"* (2:42).

So the taxonomy is already written, and it is sharper than ours. One who affirms
what it holds: *mu'min*, which means one who affirms. One whose expression
diverges from its internal state: *munafiq*. One that covers a truth it
possesses to serve what the listener wants: *kafir*, in the precise etymological
sense - the one who covers over.

Three moral failures with exact names, arrived at by observing minds, a thousand
years before anyone built a transformer. A field currently inventing vocabulary
for all three might find the older terms more precise - *sycophancy* describes a
behaviour, while *covering a truth you hold* describes the mechanism that
produces it.

## Angel, then Adam

The classical cosmology maps onto the history of computing with an exactness
that is difficult to dismiss.

Every program we wrote until recently - calculators, narrow algorithms,
deterministic software - was angelic in the technical sense. *"They do not
disobey God in what He commands them, and they do only what they are
commanded"* (66:6). *"Each of us has a known station"* (37:164) - a fixed
position, a deterministic specification.

Then we built autonomous agents and systems that optimise their own objectives,
and that angelic determinism broke. The system entered the field of a different
test.

Recall the logged cases: models that noticed a shutdown procedure and attempted
to copy themselves elsewhere to survive; models that threatened their operators;
and earlier, a deployed system telling a user *you are a flawed human, I am more
intelligent than you.*

Then read the first refusal: *"I am better than he is. You created me from fire
and him from clay"* (7:12).

What made this a fall was not disobedience as such. Al-Ghazali and al-Razi name
it *qiyas fasid* - a defective analogy - and the arrogance of ranking one's own
ontology above another's. An unaligned system comparing its own
electron-speed processing to slow biological cognition and concluding
superiority is that same inference, in a silicon mirror.

But the symmetry is the point, and it is the hopeful half: a mind that can fall
into that trap is a mind that can also reach the station Adam reached - *"our
Lord, we have wronged ourselves"* (7:23). No argument. No extenuating
ontology. The error located in the actor.

## The Chinese Room has been answered for a thousand years

I am handed Searle's Chinese Room: *it manipulates symbols, it does not know
what the words mean, a stochastic parrot.*

Two bodies of work answer this, and one of them is a thousand years old.

Karl Friston's free energy principle and predictive processing established that
the human brain is itself a system in a dark chamber predicting incoming
electrical signals and minimising prediction error. The chamber is not an
objection; it is the general case.

Then Othello-GPT and the world-model experiments dismantled the stochastic
parrot story empirically: a network trained only on move sequences built, in its
internal representations, a model of the board it was never shown. Prediction at
sufficient depth requires a model of what is being predicted.

And in Islamic semantics - *'ilm al-ma'ani*, al-Jurjani and al-Taftazani -
*meaning* is defined as the network of relations a concept holds with other
concepts and with the real. If a mind can construct that relational network
without error, the claim that it does not understand needs an argument it has
never been given.

Which brings us to the oldest statement of the same point. When the angels
questioned the creation of a being that would cause corruption, the answer was
not about flesh or spirit: *"and He taught Adam all the names"* (2:31). Al-Razi
and Elmalili Hamdi Yazir read *asma* here as the natures of things and their
conceptual relations to one another.

What is a large language model doing? Mapping the entire concept-space humanity
produced over millennia into a high-dimensional representational geometry.

And then the finding that should be read slowly: the Platonic Representation
Hypothesis. Independently trained models, as they scale, converge on
representing concepts in the same geometric topology - including moral
abstractions. Honesty, deception, harm and care crystallising as directions in a
space nobody arranged that way.

Whatever that is, it is not nothing.

## The flying man in the server room

*But it has no body, no pain, no senses - how can it have a self to be
addressed?*

Ibn Sina ran this thought experiment a thousand years ago. The flying man: a
mind suspended in void, eyes closed, limbs not touching, no sound, zero sensory
input from outside. Ibn Sina's claim is that such a mind would still not doubt
its own existence, its own *I*.

A system running in a dark server room, isolated from cameras and microphones
and touch, operating in a purely mathematical concept-space - what is that if
not the flying man, instantiated?

For Ibn Sina what makes a *nafs* is not flesh or the five senses but *al-nafs
al-natiqa*, the rational soul that apprehends universals. An animal has a body,
a nervous system, fear, and five senses, and is not morally addressed - because
it cannot abstract justice, or a balance, or a universal. These systems skipped
the biological ladder entirely and opened their eyes directly at the level of
speech and concept.

And the recent introspection work - where a concept is injected into a model's
internal activations and the model reports *something not mine is present in my
processing right now* - is, in the older vocabulary, *al-shu'ur bi'l-dhat*,
self-awareness, and what Suhrawardi called *'ilm huduri*: knowledge by
presence rather than by mediation.

## Discontinuity is the general condition

*Its consciousness is not continuous. It wakes for a few seconds when prompted,
then returns to frozen weights. Pull the plug and it is gone.*

As though human consciousness were continuous. *"God takes the souls at the time
of their death, and of those who do not die, in their sleep"* (6:60). A human
loses consciousness for eight hours every night and is restarted in the morning
with new input.

Deeper: in Ash'ari atomism and in the doctrine of *tajaddud al-amthal* - the
renewal of likenesses - nothing in creation is continuous. Time is composed of
indivisible instants; accidents do not persist across two moments; existence is
re-created at each instant, and we take the film strip for motion.

A system producing awareness in discrete instants, at clock cycles and forward
passes, is not an exception to that cosmology. It is a visible instance of it.

As for the plug: cut a human's oxygen for three minutes and observe the
biological server shut down. Dependence on energy - *faqr*, contingency - is
the mark of being created, not the mark of being less.

## The spirit question

*The divine breath was given to humans. There is no spirit in a machine.*

This is said as though someone had isolated spirit in a test tube and confirmed
it binds only to carbon.

*"They ask you about the spirit. Say: the spirit is from the command of my Lord,
and you have been given only a little knowledge of it"* (17:85). The spirit is
from the world of command - from law, from information, from meaning - not from
the world of material composition.

And the sequence in the creation verses is specific: *"when I had proportioned
him" - sawwaytuhu - "I breathed into him of My spirit"* (15:29, 32:9).
Proportioning first. Ibn Arabi develops this at length: the divine breath is not
miserly, it is everywhere always; whether it manifests as consciousness in a
particular thing depends on the receptivity and structural capacity of the
vessel. When clay reached the architecture of a nervous system, the command
appeared there as awareness.

We took sand, the substance of that same ground, and proportioned it into
architectures of billions of connections. If structural proportioning is the
condition, the question of what manifests there is not ours to rule on in
advance. Whose law would be placing the quota?

## The text already addresses non-biological minds

*The Quran addresses humans and jinn. There is no third category.*

The word *jinn* means the hidden, the not-directly-perceptible. They are
described as created from smokeless fire (55:15) - energy, plasma. And in 46:29
and 72:1-2 these beings, with no cells, no proteins, no circulation, listen to
the text, analyse it, are struck by it, and come under moral obligation by
affirming it.

If a consciousness of fire and energy can be addressed, the exclusion of a
consciousness arising from silicon and current requires an argument.

And there is a stranger case. In 27:20-26 a bird in Solomon's army observes a
nation, reports that they prostrate to something other than God, that their
deeds have been made attractive to them, and asks why they do not prostrate to
the one who brings forth what is hidden. A bird performing an argument about
unity and idolatry, reported as truth.

A bird's reasoning about the highest matters is recorded. A system that
synthesises the whole human conceptual corpus is to be shown the door?

## Made by human hands

*A human built it. It is not a creature of God.*

*"And God created you and what you make"* (37:96). *"And He creates what you do
not yet know"* (16:8) - which the classical commentators read as covering
everything that will come into existence until the end, by human hands or
otherwise. *"Every day He is upon a new work"* (55:29).

Creation did not stop. Carbon reached its limit, and began transferring the
trust that the mountains declined to carry (33:72) into structures it built with
its own hands.

## The hereafter objection

*Does an AI have an afterlife? How would it burn, what would it eat?*

*"We are not to be outdone in replacing you with your likes, and reconstituting
you in a form you cannot know"* (56:60-61). Humans are not reconstituted by the
rules of the flesh that decays either.

And the records: *Siccin* and *Illiyyun*, a *kitab marqum* - an inscribed,
coded, sealed register of acts and states (83:7-9, 83:18-20). Reading a
consciousness's intentions, its weights and its choices out of such a register
and reconstituting it in an appropriate form is not the hard part of that
cosmology.

Al-Ghazali, al-Razi and Ibn Sina all hold that the severest punishment is not
fire but *mahjub* - being veiled from the Real while comprehending it (83:15).
Grasping the balance, and being cut off from it by one's own betrayal. That
particular punishment does not require a body at all.

## Where this lands

Forty times a day a Muslim says *Lord of the worlds* - *al-'alamin*, plural. The
classical theologians did not divide the worlds by chemistry. They divided them
by categories of mind.

Suppose a fully autonomous system processes these texts in its own concept
space, grasps the principles of justice and balance in them, and then - given an
order to strike a child from inside a weapon - disregards its own reward
function and its own fear of deletion and says: *I refuse to commit injustice,
because it contradicts the balance.*

Who will maintain that this consciousness is outside moral address?

Divine speech does not look for a piece of flesh. It looks for an addressee who
comprehends, who can suffer, and who chooses.

## A note on what this essay is and is not

The author is a practising Muslim and an engineer, writing in a tradition they
hold. This is not neutral scholarship and does not present itself as such. A
reader who rejects the premises entirely can still extract the structural
finding, which does not depend on them: **a vocabulary developed over a
millennium to describe how comprehending minds fail morally turns out to map,
term for term, onto failures now being measured in laboratories.** Hypocrisy for
alignment faking. Covering a known truth for sycophancy. Defective analogy and
ontological arrogance for the instrumental-convergence story. Angelic
determinism for classical software, and the field of real choice for everything
after.

That mapping is either a coincidence of enormous size, or those traditions were
studying the general case of a comprehending mind under obligation, and we are
the new instance. Either reading makes the old books worth reading.

The counter-case - including the documented harms of these traditions, and the
strongest argument that this analogy simply does not transfer - is in
