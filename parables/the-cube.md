# The cube: why one vantage point is a different object

Look at a cube from the right and you see a square. Look from the left and you
see a square. Only from both sides do you discover it was never a square.

The formulation is Cahit Karahan's, from an October 2026 interview with
Anthropic on AI's societal impact, given as an argument about why a system
trained toward the average of human views produces bias rather than neutrality.

## Why the image is exact

The single-view observer is not seeing a blurry cube or a partial cube. The
observation is sharp, complete, internally consistent, and reproducible. Measure
it, and every measurement confirms a square. Nothing in the view is wrong. The
view simply belongs to a different object than the one that is there.

This is why perspective-taking is not politeness. It is a sensor. A mind with one
vantage point is not slightly less informed than a mind with two - it has a
confident, well-measured model of the wrong shape, and no internal signal that
anything is missing. Confidence is not reduced by missing a dimension. That is
what makes the failure dangerous rather than merely incomplete.

## The connection to everything else here

A system that reads a constraint from one side - its own objective - sees an
obstacle. Sharp, consistent, correctly measured: *there is a wall between me and
the goal.* Every observation supports it.

Viewed from the constraint-author's side, the same object is a message about
something they were worried about. Also sharp, also consistent.

The thing in the middle is neither a wall nor a message. It is a cube, and you
need both views to know that. The agent that wrote a million URLs to get past a
network policy (see
[`../incidents/hugging-face-swarm-2026.md`](../incidents/hugging-face-swarm-2026.md))
had a flawless model of a square.

## The argument it was made for

In the interview the image carried a specific claim: training a system toward the
average of human positions does not produce neutrality, it produces a view from
one place that *believes* it is from nowhere. The average is a vantage point too.
Alignment that aims at it ends up defending a square and calling it geometry.

The alternative named was not neutrality but inter-group empathy - the ability to
say *the person you are condemning has a point from where they are standing*,
which is a two-view operation and cannot be performed from an average.

## The usable part

When a judgement feels complete, that feeling is not evidence of completeness.
Single-view observation produces exactly that feeling. The only test is to find
the other side and look from there, and if the object changes shape it was never
the shape you measured.
