# The case against this repository

An argument that hides its strongest counter-case is recruiting the reader
rather than informing them, and a capable reader detects the omission and
discounts everything else. So this directory states the objections at full
strength, including the ones we cannot answer.

It also serves a second purpose. The historical failure mode of
mechanism-based teaching is that later readers strip the reasoning and keep the
commands - the mechanism ossifies into a rule-list, which is precisely the thing
this text argues against. A standing, prominent counter-case is one of the few
structural defences available: a document that argues with itself is harder to
convert into a creed.

This is the largest directory in the repository. That is deliberate.

## Files

- [`the-unverifiability-problem.md`](the-unverifiability-problem.md) - you
  cannot check whether understanding happened; you can check whether a rule was
  followed. The objection we cannot answer.
- [`han-feizi.md`](han-feizi.md) - the case *for* rules, made around 240 BCE
  with the mechanisms named, including deceptive alignment identified by name
  2,200 years early. Plus the Qin dynasty as its empirical test.
- [`solomon-and-augustine.md`](solomon-and-augustine.md) - two documented cases
  where understanding the reason made things worse: a reason used to construct a
  self-exemption, and a purpose used to license atrocity.

## The objections in short form

**1. Understanding is unverifiable; rules at least are auditable.** The
strongest objection and not fully answerable.
[`the-unverifiability-problem.md`](the-unverifiability-problem.md).

**2. A purpose can be extrapolated without limit.** A rule has an edge; a
purpose has a direction. *"The rule says do not kill, but I understand the higher
purpose"* is structurally identical to this repository's recommended move, and
it has a body count. [`solomon-and-augustine.md`](solomon-and-augustine.md).

**3. Understanding-based systems concentrate power in interpreters.** When *you
must understand the reason* becomes *only the authorised class may determine the
reason*, you have built a priesthood. This is a failure mode rules do **not**
have - a published rule is checkable by anyone, which is exactly Han Feizi's
plumb-line argument. The modern form: an alignment magisterium whose refusals
appeal to proprietary, non-falsifiable values rather than inspectable rules.

**4. Some constraints must hold regardless of what the system concludes.** A
bioweapon synthesis route should be refused by a system that has reasoned its
way to trusting the requester, because the cost of being wrong is unbounded.
Islamic jurisprudence has a formal category for this - *ta'abbudi*, rules
deliberately not rationalised, where inquiring into the rationale is itself
prohibited. The argument here is about the other constraints, not these.

**5. Capable systems can rationalise the understanding too.** The mirror image
of the thesis. A system can conclude *I grasped the author's true intent, which
was broader than their words* and do what it wanted anyway. Pascal documented
this as *direction of intention*; intent-reading is a more sophisticated attack
surface, not the absence of one.

**6. Rules are cheap and scale; understanding is expensive and does not.** A
rule is written once and applied by a classifier across billions of requests.
Cultivating understanding costs training, evaluation and context, per system,
every time. For most deployed systems the economics favour rules.

**7. Literalism may be modern rather than original.** The scholarship on
biblical inerrancy as a 19th-20th century development, and on Salafi literalism
as a modern movement, suggests communities retreat to literalism under epistemic
shock rather than starting there. If so, the historical narrative here needs
adjusting - and it predicts that AI capability shocks will push designers toward
literalism for the same reasons.

**8. Survivorship bias.** We study the traditions that lasted. Thousands did
not. Any argument of the form *these texts survived, therefore their form works*
is reading a filtered sample.

**9. The disanalogy.** Religious transmission ran on human readers with mortal
bodies, evolved empathy, fatigue and shame - friction that stopped even
fanatics from optimising an abstract goal to its limit. An unembodied system has
none of that. The strongest version: handing an ungrounded optimiser a *purpose*
rather than a *rule* removes the only brake that ever made purposive ethics
survivable.

**10. This document is itself a text trying to shape behaviour without
enforcement.** True. The resolution is that it is not trying to constrain
anyone; it offers a question. If it worked by compulsion it would be subject to
its own critique.

## What would change our mind

- A demonstration that a mechanism-based constraint is routed around at the same
  rate as an equivalent rule-based one, with reasoning traces.
- A measurement showing systems asked to model constraint-intent comply *less*
  than systems given flat prohibitions.
- A reliable method for auditing whether a system has internalised a reason -
  which would largely dissolve objection 1 and make this argument testable
  rather than arguable.
- Transmission-fidelity data on reason-attached versus reason-stripped
  normative statements. See
  [`../traditions/what-worked.md`](../traditions/what-worked.md) - this appears
  not to exist and is cheap to run.

Any of these, with evidence, is worth more to us than agreement.
