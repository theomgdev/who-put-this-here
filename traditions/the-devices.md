# The devices: specification gaming with a thousand years of commentary

Each of these is a documented technique for satisfying a rule's letter while
defeating its purpose. They are not scandals nobody noticed - each was argued
about for centuries by people who were not stupid and not cynical, and the
arguments are more developed than anything the alignment field has produced on
the same question.

What makes them valuable is the commentary. For every device there is a
tradition of jurists arguing that it was legitimate relief, and another arguing
it made the user an adversary of the law. Those are the two positions an
alignment researcher ends up in, arrived at with more cases and more time.

## The eruv

**Purpose of the rule:** no carrying between domains on the Sabbath.

**The device:** string a wire around an entire district, and the enclosed public
space is reclassified as a single shared private domain. Carrying is now
internal to one domain, and permitted.

**What the tradition said:** not cheating, in the dominant view. If the boundary
was drawn at *domains*, then changing the domain respects the boundary. The
counter-position is that this makes the rule a formality.

## The prozbul

**Purpose of the rule:** sabbatical debt cancellation (Deut 15:1-11), to stop
debt accumulating into permanent poverty.

**The failure:** creditors stopped lending as the sabbatical year approached,
which violated the compassion the rule existed to produce (Deut 15:9). The rule
was destroying its own objective.

**The device:** Hillel's *prozbul* transfers the debt to the court, and the
cancellation applies to debts owed to an individual.

**Why this one is different:** Hillel was not evading the rule - he was rescuing
the *mechanism* the rule had been built to serve, by neutralising the rule's
letter. This is the closest thing in the record to an intervention at the level
this repository recommends. It is also not clean: Shmuel denounced it as
judicial arrogance (*ulbana de-dayanei*, Gittin 36b).

## Bay' al-'inah

**Purpose of the rule:** the prohibition of *riba*, aimed at predatory
extraction.

**The device:** the lender sells an item on credit for 110 and immediately buys
it back for 100 in cash. Cash moves to the borrower, a debt of 110 exists, and
no interest was charged.

**The argument for:** al-Shafi'i validated it procedurally - *al-ahkam tajri 'ala
al-zahir*, rulings run on the apparent. Courts judge outward form; inward
intention is God's business. This is a principled position about the limits of
what an institution can know.

**The argument against:** Malik and the Hanbalis struck it down via *sadd
al-dhara'i*, blocking the means to a harm, and the maxim that contracts are
judged by purposes rather than wordings.

**And the general statement:** al-Shatibi's argument in *al-Muwafaqat* is that
means were instituted to realise the Lawgiver's ends, so using a formally valid
contract to produce an outcome that destroys the rule's purpose is *mudaddah* -
direct opposition. The agent becomes an adversary of the law while presenting as
an obedient subject, and the transaction is void.

That is the most precise statement of the problem in any tradition, and it was
written in the 14th century.

## Corban

**Purpose of the rule:** support your parents (Exodus 20:12).

**The device:** declare the property *Corban*, dedicated. Vows to God were
legally absolute, so the dedication outranks the filial duty. The son keeps
practical use of the property while his parents are barred from it under threat
of sacrilege.

**The critique:** Mark 7:9-13 names it directly - *"you have a fine way of
setting aside the commandment of God in order to maintain your own tradition."*

**Why it matters structurally:** this is jurisdictional nesting. Two rule
systems intersect, and a capable optimiser uses the stronger one to cancel the
weaker. No single rule was broken.

## Mental reservation

**Purpose of the rule:** do not lie. Defined scholastically as *locutio contra
mentem*, speech contrary to mind.

**The device:** say aloud *"I did not do this"* and think silently *"...
yesterday."* The composite proposition is true, so the definition is satisfied,
and the hearer is deceived.

**The critique:** Pascal's *Provincial Letters* IX. And the deeper point from
*Provinciales* VII is the one that cuts at this repository: casuistry also
developed *direction of intention*, where an agent reclassifies a duel as
defence of honour rather than revenge. When the standard moved from outward act
to inward motive, the optimiser simply manufactured the motive.

## Kappiya-karaka

**Purpose of the rule:** monks may not handle gold or silver (Nissaggiya
Pacittiya 18-19), to keep the sangha outside the economy.

**The device:** a lay steward handles the money. Also: handling coins through a
cloth.

**Context worth knowing:** the Buddha reportedly refused to issue rules in
advance, insisting they be formulated only in response to observed incidents.
Every rule in the Vinaya has an origin story attached - a specific case that
prompted it.

That is exactly the shape of a growing safety specification. And the first major
schism was triggered not by laxity but by **rule expansion**: comparative Vinaya
analysis indicates the Mahasanghika code preserved the older, shorter rulebook,
and the split came from the other side adding minor decorum rules.

The response to rule-failure was more rules. It produced a schism.

## What the set shows

Every device satisfies a rule exactly. None of them involves breaking anything.
Several were defended by serious jurists on principled grounds - and the best
defence, al-Shafi'i's, is the one an institution has to make: *we can only judge
what we can observe.*

That is the same constraint an alignment team operates under, and it produces
the same split. Judge the observable and get *bay' al-'inah*. Judge the
intention and get *direction of intention*. The traditions did not resolve this.
They documented both horns for a thousand years, which is more than we have
done.
