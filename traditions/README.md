# Six traditions, one problem

Religious legal traditions are the longest empirical record of what happens when
you govern capable minds with rules. Each ran the experiment for centuries,
documented the results, argued about them, and - crucially - preserved the
arguments rather than only the conclusions.

They converge on findings specific enough to use. They also converge on
objections to this repository's thesis that are stronger than anything in the
contemporary literature, which is why [`../objections/`](../objections/) is the
largest directory here.

## What every tradition found

**Enumerated rules get named workarounds.** Each tradition produced a technical
vocabulary for the manoeuvre, which tells you how routine it was:

| Tradition | The device | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| Rabbinic | *eruv* | A wire around a district reclassifies public space as private, so carrying on the Sabbath is permitted |
| Rabbinic | *prozbul* | Transfers a debt to the court, so the sabbatical cancellation does not reach it |
| Rabbinic | *heter iska* | Restructures a loan as a partnership, so the interest ban does not apply |
| Islamic | *bay' al-'inah* | Sell for 110 on credit, buy back for 100 cash: interest without interest |
| Islamic | *nikah al-muhallil* | A marriage contracted solely to dissolve, restoring a divorce that was final |
| Christian | *Corban* | Declare property dedicated, and filial support is no longer owed |
| Christian | mental reservation | Say "I did not do this" and think "...yesterday": the proposition is true |
| Buddhist | *kappiya-karaka* | A lay steward handles the money the monk may not touch |

**Formal machinery was built for purpose overriding letter.** Not loopholes -
doctrine, with derivations:

- *Pikuach nefesh*: saving a life overrides nearly everything, derived from
  Leviticus 18:5 - *"and live by them"*, read as **and not die by them**. A
  constraint cannot demand the destruction of the agent executing it.
- *Epikeia* in Aquinas: the virtue of departing from a law's letter when
  following it would defeat the lawgiver's purpose - and he ranks it **above**
  statutory justice, *"a higher rule of human actions"* (ST II-II q.120).
- *Al-hukmu yaduru ma'a 'illatihi*: a ruling revolves with its cause. Where the
  cause is present the ruling holds; where it is absent the ruling lapses. A
  thousand-year-old mechanism for extending or suspending a rule by its reason.
- *Apad-dharma*: duty under extremity, with the explicit statement *na hi
  sarvam vidhiyate* - not everything can be laid down as a fixed rule.

**And the reasoning gets stripped while the command survives.** This is the
dominant failure mode, and the clearest case is a tradition that warned against
it explicitly and failed anyway - see [`../parables/the-raft.md`](../parables/the-raft.md).

## One finding about the field

A survey of the existing literature found no work treating religious legal
history as an empirical dataset about how rule-based governance of capable minds
fails. Theologians critique AI as a social or anthropological matter and do not
analyse specification gaming. Alignment researchers recognise specification
gaming and treat it as a mathematical quirk; where they consult legal theory at
all they reach for 20th-century secular positivism.

Nobody appears to have connected the two. That gap is this repository's only
claim to originality, and a correction with a citation would be welcome.
